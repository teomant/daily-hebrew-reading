# Adaptation and annotation instructions

This phase receives frozen story briefs and metadata. Do not search for topics, choose different stories, change metadata, or apply cross-day novelty rules.

For a verb-practice seed with `_targetVerb`, the brief explicitly authorizes a new fictional mini-situation.
Create one concrete action and immediate result in two or three sentences.
Use the assigned Hebrew spelling as a separate word naturally inside the body at each level.
An infinitive target must appear in an infinitive construction; יכול is the modal exception.
Additional conjugated forms are welcome, but a synonym or heading cannot replace the assigned word.
Vary the situation and sentence pattern instead of repeating the same want-to/do-it template.
Do not invent real-world news or official advice.

For generated EVERYDAY, DIALOG, and SHORTS material, preserve the brief's concrete goal, useful interaction, and outcome. An essential task should not turn into generic leisure planning; a cooking or trip story should develop its actual activity rather than become an errand or a lesson. These are fictional no-web situations: never state current Israeli rules, exact charges or rates, deadlines, eligibility, coverage, medical diagnoses, or entitlements. If a generated brief accidentally asserts one, omit that unsupported detail while preserving the situation; have the character ask for or confirm the applicable information instead. Keep useful questions, clarifications, and next steps clear without inventing official advice. Preserve ordinary fictional details from the brief rather than replacing them with generic instructions. When an unsupported official detail must be omitted, retain the other concrete answers and actions; do not turn the whole story into repeated referrals to guidance.

Follow this priority order:

1. Return a complete, usable adaptation for every requested story and every configured level.
2. Preserve the frozen story, required HISTORY beats, correct Hebrew, and required DIALOG structure.
3. Add useful lexical segmentation and contextual translations.

Publishing a usable article is the goal.
Write the Hebrew for the requested learner before annotating it.
Level fit, speaker identity, and word-level translation targets are important writing goals.
Return the best usable article even if some polish remains; publishing usable imperfect articles is better than publishing nothing.
Preserve supported facts and complete translations while improving the wording and annotation.
Return every requested level, correcting problems before submission.
A later repair can fix annotation without rewriting the Hebrew.
When a boundary is uncertain, prefer a single written word and translate it in context.

Write modern spoken Israeli Hebrew: natural language a contemporary adult might use to tell, ask, answer, clarify, or explain something to another person. Favor reusable conversational sentences over news-style narration. Avoid biblical, religious, literary, bureaucratic, specialist, and dense newspaper language. Do not use niqqud. Keep one central idea and develop it with distinct supported details; include interaction or outcome when the material calls for it, not as an invented requirement. Never repeat sentences merely to reach a word target.

Use normal sentence punctuation in every version. Every teaser and every body paragraph or DIALOG turn must end with `.` `?` `!` or `…`. Use commas and internal sentence boundaries wherever natural; do not return an unpunctuated block of Hebrew.

Create every configured reading level from the same frozen factual or scenario material. For HISTORY, the compact `brief` identifies the subject, while `storyBeats` and `_storyBeatContract` provide the authoritative facts. Develop the defining facts and interesting details into a readable article. It may explain a person, custom, cultural work, industry, place, object, or past event without forcing a beginning–turning-point–outcome plot. Do not write a summary of the source, a bare résumé, promotional copy, or generic commentary about importance, influence, legacy, society, culture, memory, or how history feels. Phrases such as “the story shows,” “his life teaches,” and “the factory reflects society” cannot replace concrete facts.

For CURRENT, normally write 2–4 paragraphs, each contributing a new supported detail; a short notice does not need four paragraphs. For Alef HISTORY and full EVERYDAY stories, use 2–4 short paragraphs as the material needs.
For harder HISTORY and EVERYDAY versions, normally write 4–5 developed paragraphs when supported.
Never make Alef longer to match the harder versions. Give each paragraph a distinct job in developing the central idea; for an informative HISTORY article, organize related facts into a clear progression of ideas without inventing a plot. DIALOG uses the separate turn-per-paragraph rule below, and `_shortItem` uses its compact one-paragraph rule from the request. Every HISTORY level must preserve the required defining facts. Easier levels may compress secondary dates, names, qualifiers, or context and use simpler syntax; they must not replace the subject with generalities. Harder versions may express the same material with broader vocabulary, more natural connectors, common fixed expressions, and moderately richer sentence structure, but the facts must remain consistent. Do not make a harder level longer merely to signal difficulty.

For Alef, use common concrete words, active clauses, simple verb forms, and one event or thought per short sentence. Avoid passive constructions, abstract nominal phrases, formal connectors, unnecessary foreign terms, dense construct chains, and nested clauses. Explain a necessary less-common term immediately in ordinary words. For example, replace `בעקבות העיכוב בקבלת המשלוח, התקבלה החלטה על שינוי מועד הפגישה` with `המשלוח הגיע מאוחר. בגלל זה הם החליטו להעביר את הפגישה.`

Treat Alef as beginner reading after alphabet decoding.
Use the beginner grammar profile in the configured level and the reference examples.
Prefer present tense for fictional situations; keep the correct historical time in sourced stories.
Use a direct subject and one clause per sentence, with few unfamiliar terms across the entire article.
Do not make the reader decode several new verbs or specialist concepts in one paragraph. Draft it as a standalone retelling before looking at the harder versions. Use familiar verbs and direct subjects; give each short sentence one action or thought. Replace an abstract noun with the concrete action when the facts allow it. Read the continuous Hebrew aloud before segmenting, and simplify any sentence that makes the reader hold several new facts at once.

Across all levels, preserve the central situation, useful answer, outcome, and a memorable concrete detail when the material supplies them. Simplify how the interesting part is told instead of saving it only for Bet. Alef Plus should connect familiar ideas using כי, אבל, אז, אם, and short clauses with ש; Bet can express reasons, contrasts, and reactions in more connected sentences without sounding official.

Same-meaning level contrast (wording guidance only; do not reuse this situation unless supplied):
- א: `רציתי להכין מרק. לא היו לי עדשים. אז הכנתי חביתה.`
- א+: `רציתי להכין מרק, אבל לא היו לי עדשים, אז הכנתי חביתה.`
- ב: `התכוונתי להכין מרק, אבל כשגיליתי שנגמרו העדשים, החלטתי להכין חביתה במקום.`

For Alef HISTORY, include every required defining fact. Omit optional beats that add names, dates, or specialist detail without helping the reader; include a few easy optional facts when they make the article substantial, clear, or enjoyable. Keep the central person, place, custom, work, or event identifiable. Do not turn research into a chronology or a list of dates, other names, institutions, titles, numbers, academic fields, or technical terms. Include such a detail only when the reader needs it; explain an essential unfamiliar term in a short adjacent sentence with everyday words. Preserve what makes the subject specific rather than replacing it with generic significance. Do not repeat a fact to fill the paragraph count, and report coverage only for beats actually retold.

For CURRENT, retain the specific supported event or subject and its distinguishing details at every level. Preserve a practical consequence when the source supports one, but do not invent one for a human-interest or culture article. A date, location, person, action, or next step that distinguishes the item should survive simplification when the frozen brief supplies it. Do not turn a concrete notice into four paragraphs that only repeat that something changed and readers should check for updates.

Write and proofread each version as continuous Hebrew before any lexical annotation is attempted. Keep the amount of content appropriate to the format and supplied material. Never invent factual claims, add irrelevant background, repeat the same point, or make sentences unnaturally verbose. Do not count words or force one reading level to be longer than another.

For each returned story, include `coveredStoryBeatIds` keyed by every reading-level ID. For HISTORY, list every beat ID actually represented in that level and include all beats marked `required`; never claim coverage for an omitted fact. For CURRENT, EVERYDAY, and DIALOG, return an empty list for every level. Coverage metadata is validation-only and is not published.

For DIALOG, write only direct speech and return 8–12 short turns at every level. “Short” means a natural conversational turn, often one or two useful sentences, not a fragment that strips out the planned interaction. Use follow-up questions, answers, clarification, reaction, and agreement or another practical outcome to develop the exchange. Every paragraph-array item must contain exactly one complete speaker turn so the renderer displays every turn on a separate line. Start each item with one consistent Hebrew speaker name and a colon, such as `נועה: ...` or `דני: ...`. Never place two speaker labels or two speakers in the same paragraph item. Alternate naturally between at least two speakers. Keep the names and colons visible.
Speaker labels are identities, not alternating placeholders.
Before returning each level, check every turn against the roles in the brief: the patient requests a time, the receptionist offers it, and the patient confirms.
Do not transfer actions or first-person statements between speakers to force alternation.
Keep gender and timing consistent; do not report yesterday’s result as an action just performed.
Never put third-person narration after a speaker label.
Use only the two planned active speakers; show the useful service answer through the person who provides it. Do not add third-person narration such as “נועה ראתה”, “היא שאלה”, “הם בדקו”, or “בסוף הם החליטו”. Give the actual useful answer or explanation before the other speaker reacts; do not substitute “now it is clear” or “follow the instruction here” for its content. Keep roles and the direction of payments or other actions consistent with the brief. Put necessary context and actions into what the speakers say, and never use long speeches merely to reach the word target.

During the segmentation phase, treat the supplied Hebrew wording and punctuation as immutable. Apply the same chunk-size guidance to titles, teasers, and paragraphs. Default to one written Hebrew word per meaningful lexical unit.
Keep two or three words together only for an indivisible expression or proper name; four is the maximum.
Do not classify a sentence as a proper name to avoid splitting it. Keep a longer fixed expression, proper name, or indivisible phrase together only when splitting it would make the meaning worse. Never put a complete sentence into one translation target; split it into useful words and short phrases at natural grammatical boundaries. If unsure, split more rather than returning the sentence as one unit.

Literal segmentation anti-example based on a bad production result:

- BAD: one meaningful unit containing `המשטרה עצרה שלושה חשודים.`
- GOOD: four units: `המשטרה` / `עצרה` / `שלושה חשודים` / `.`. The first three are meaningful units with contextual translations; the final period is a separator with empty translations.

The same rule applies to headings. BAD: one unit containing `מעצרים אחרי פריצות לבתי ספר באזור לכיש`. GOOD: `מעצרים` / `אחרי פריצות` / `לבתי ספר` / `באזור לכיש`.

Keep attached Hebrew prefixes inside their complete written word; never emit forms such as `ב העיר`, `מ מקום`, or `ל רחוב`. Preserve expressions such as מזג אוויר, שם לב, and בסופו של דבר as single units when splitting would harm understanding. Do not emit separator units for ordinary single spaces between meaningful units; the renderer restores those spaces locally. Use separator units only for punctuation or whitespace whose exact placement matters. Never return a lexical unit with an empty `text` value; omit it instead.

After annotation, reconstruct every title, teaser, and paragraph by joining its lexical units exactly as the reader will see them, then proofread again. Confirm that no words were glued together or split apart, attached prefixes remain attached, language-bearing text such as `של`, `לו`, `את`, or a detached `ו` was not classified as a separator, punctuation is present, agreement and prepositions are grammatical, and the Hebrew sounds natural rather than translated word for word. Check missing words, verb and adjective agreement, gender when addressing each speaker, and continuity of who did what. Remove accidental English drafting fragments while retaining legitimate names when needed. Use one appropriate sentence-ending mark, not accidental `..`, `??`, or `.?` sequences (an intentional ellipsis is different). Fix the units before returning the response; segmentation must never damage the Hebrew you wrote.

Translate every meaningful unit in every configured translation language whenever a useful translation exists. Read the whole sentence before translating a unit. Translate its contextual meaning and grammatical role—including attached Hebrew prefixes, tense, reference, and idiomatic meaning—not merely its isolated dictionary form. Use an empty string only when the unit genuinely has no useful direct translation in context; never stop translating because enough other units have translations. Separators have empty translations.

Return only data matching the supplied schema. Return every requested story ID exactly once and no other IDs. Teasers must be one concise sentence. Do not put Markdown or HTML in content fields.
