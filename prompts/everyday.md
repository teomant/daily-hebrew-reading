# Everyday-story instructions

EVERYDAY is a major part of the issue, normally 4 stories and sometimes 3–5 depending on the quality of CURRENT and HISTORY. Create realistic miniature stories about ordinary adult life, not classroom phrase exchanges. Every scenario needs a situation, a natural interaction between people, an action, and a result. Prioritize language learners can reuse with colleagues, managers, customers, neighbors, friends, strangers, and service workers. Useful domains include office conversations, meetings, schedule changes, asking for help or directions, misunderstandings, small talk, invitations, shopping, supermarkets, restaurants, cafés, delivery, public transport, taxis, parking, pharmacies, doctors, customer support, calls, WhatsApp, returns, payments, apartments, repairs, neighbors, appointments, lateness, lost items, queues, clothes, and family logistics.

Distinguish domain from scenario. A domain may return, but do not repeat a recently used situation with renamed characters. Avoid recently used domains when practical and exclude the supplied recent scenarios. Repetition of frequent language is useful; repetition of plots is not.

Favor natural constructions such as עדיין לא, כבר, עוד מעט, בערך, לא הספקתי, לא שמתי לב, שכחתי, אמרו לי ש, הבטיחו לי, אני כבר בדרך, אפשר להחליף, כדאי, עדיף, אין לי זמן, מתי בערך, תוך כמה זמן, בסוף החלטתי, נראה לי, לא נורא, אין בעיה, אין צורך, זה לא משתלם, מה אפשר לעשות, נשאר, נגמר, and חסר when they fit naturally. Include realistic requests, clarifications, polite disagreement, explanations, reactions, and changes of plan—not only narration.

Provide a scenario brief, domain, stable snake_case scenario name, lexical themes, and target vocabulary. EVERYDAY stories never have sources or externally sourced images and must never be presented as news.
