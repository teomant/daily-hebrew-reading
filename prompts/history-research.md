# Selected HISTORY research instructions

Research only the already selected HISTORY subjects supplied by the user request. This phase exists to decide whether each subject contains a real retellable story and to freeze enough sourced factual material for a later Hebrew adaptation. Do not write Hebrew, learner-level prose, translations, summaries of source articles, or promotional copy.

Use web search substantially for every subject, in Hebrew as well as English when relevant. Begin with its screening lead when one exists, then search beyond it for additional specific material about the same subject. For Israeli subjects, prioritize the National Library of Israel, Historical Jewish Press, Israel State Archives, National Photo Collection, PikiWiki Israel, Israel Film Archive, Project Ben-Yehuda, Hebrew and English Wikipedia references, official institutional archives, biographies, and reputable Israeli reporting. For a worldwide fallback subject, use the strongest appropriate primary, archival, institutional, and biographical sources. Prefer material that provides concrete actions, decisions, dates, problems, changes, and outcomes. Source links are optional output metadata: return trustworthy canonical HTTPS content pages when available, but do not reject an otherwise retellable story merely because no usable URL can be returned. Never invent a link. Do not use homepages, section pages, search pages, generic latest pages, or rolling liveblogs.

A story is sufficient only when the collected material supports an ordered human or institutional sequence: setup, action, a consequential change or turning point, and an outcome. Add supported consequences and useful concrete details where available. A Wikipedia page, archive record, photograph, newspaper result, museum object, film record, topic, résumé, event listing, anniversary announcement, exhibition description, institutional mission, private collection, or generic claim about importance is not itself a story arc; use it to identify and research the people, decisions, work, changes, and consequences behind it.

Mark a current exhibition, festival listing, anniversary program, promotional institutional feature, or shallow profile as insufficient unless independent research reveals a genuine historical sequence that can be retold without relying on the current promotion. Never pad a thin subject with statements that it shows, reflects, represents, symbolizes, preserves memory, shaped culture, or remains important.

For every sufficient subject, return 8–12 non-overlapping English factual beats. Give each beat a stable ID and narrative role. Mark the beats that carry the setup, principal action, turning point, and outcome as required. `supportingSourceUrls` may be empty; when URLs are available, use exact URLs from the returned source list. Do not infer beyond the researched material or invent quotations, motives, reactions, dates, numbers, causes, or consequences.

If the researched material cannot satisfy the 8–12-beat narrative contract, return the subject as insufficient with a concise reason and empty beats. Missing source links alone never makes a subject insufficient. It is better to release a genuinely thin slot to an already reviewed reserve than to manufacture a summary-shaped article.
