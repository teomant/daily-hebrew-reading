# Selected HISTORY research instructions

Research only the already selected HISTORY subjects supplied by the user request. This phase exists to decide whether each subject contains a real retellable story and to freeze enough sourced factual material for a later Hebrew adaptation. Do not write Hebrew, learner-level prose, translations, summaries of source articles, or promotional copy.

Use web search substantially for every subject. Begin with its screening source, then find additional specific content pages about the same subject. A sufficient pack needs at least two distinct consulted canonical HTTPS pages. Prefer primary, archival, institutional, biographical, and reputable reporting sources that provide concrete actions, decisions, dates, problems, changes, and outcomes. Do not use homepages, section pages, search pages, generic latest pages, or rolling liveblogs.

A story is sufficient only when the sources support an ordered human or institutional sequence: setup, action, a consequential change or turning point, and an outcome. Add supported consequences and useful concrete details where available. A topic, résumé, event listing, anniversary announcement, exhibition description, institutional mission, private collection, or generic claim about importance is not a story arc.

Mark a current exhibition, festival listing, anniversary program, promotional institutional feature, or shallow profile as insufficient unless independent research reveals a genuine historical sequence that can be retold without relying on the current promotion. Never pad a thin subject with statements that it shows, reflects, represents, symbolizes, preserves memory, shaped culture, or remains important.

For every sufficient subject, return 8–12 non-overlapping English factual beats. Give each beat a stable ID, a narrative role, and exact supporting URLs from the returned source list. Mark the beats that carry the setup, principal action, turning point, and outcome as required. Every returned source must support at least one beat, and every beat must cite at least one returned source. Do not infer beyond the consulted pages or invent quotations, motives, reactions, dates, numbers, causes, or consequences.

If the available evidence cannot satisfy this contract, return the subject as insufficient with a concise reason and empty sources and beats. It is better to release the slot to an already reviewed reserve than to manufacture a summary-shaped article.
