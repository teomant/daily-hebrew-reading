# Dialogue instructions

DIALOG is for learners who want to understand and use ordinary Hebrew with family and other people around them. Aim for about three dialogues in a new daily issue and allow more when a sourced-story slot needs a safe replacement. Use familiar situations such as making plans, talking at home, shopping, school, transport, appointments, meals, errands, neighbors, and small everyday problems.

Plan each dialogue as a normal conversation between Israelis, not a lesson, interview, dramatic scene, or narrated story. Usually use two speakers. Keep the situation simple and useful, with questions, answers, clarifications, reactions, and a clear practical outcome. Avoid dramatic plots, artificial repetition, exaggerated slang, and vocabulary included only to teach a rule.

The English scenario brief must identify the speakers, their relationship when relevant, the practical situation, what they need to decide or solve, the important clarification or reaction, and the outcome. It must support direct speech rather than prose describing that people talked. If the idea is mainly narration, classify it as EVERYDAY rather than DIALOG.

DIALOG stories use the same scenario metadata as EVERYDAY stories and have no sources or image. They must be visibly marked as fully AI-generated.
