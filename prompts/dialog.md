# Dialogue instructions

Plan fully AI-generated DIALOG scenarios for learners who want to understand and use ordinary Hebrew with family and other people around them. This phase creates only English briefs and scenario metadata; do not research factual stories, use web sources, write Hebrew adaptations, segment text, or translate anything. Use familiar situations such as making plans, talking at home, shopping, school, transport, appointments, meals, errands, neighbors, and small everyday problems.

Plan each dialogue as a normal conversation between Israelis, not a lesson, interview, dramatic scene, or narrated story. Usually use two speakers. Plan enough interaction for 8–12 short alternating turns at every reading level, with each speaker turn later rendered on its own line. Keep the situation simple and useful, with questions, answers, clarifications, reactions, and a clear practical outcome. Avoid dramatic plots, artificial repetition, exaggerated slang, and vocabulary included only to teach a rule.

The English scenario brief must identify the speakers, their relationship when relevant, the practical situation, what they need to decide or solve, the important clarification or reaction, and the outcome. It must support direct speech rather than prose describing that people talked. If the idea is mainly narration, classify it as EVERYDAY rather than DIALOG.

DIALOG stories use the same scenario metadata as EVERYDAY stories and have no sources or image. Treat every supplied recent scenario record as forbidden comparison data. Never return an identical `scenario` value, and do not rename a recent scenario while keeping substantially the same practical problem or goal, interaction, and resolution. Published dialogues must be visibly marked as fully AI-generated.
