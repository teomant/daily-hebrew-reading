# Dialogue instructions

DIALOG is for learners who want to understand and use ordinary Hebrew with family and other people around them. Aim for about three dialogues in a new daily issue and allow more when a sourced-story slot needs a safe replacement. Use familiar situations such as making plans, talking at home, shopping, school, transport, appointments, meals, errands, neighbors, and small everyday problems.

Make each dialogue sound like a normal conversation between Israelis, not a lesson, interview, dramatic scene, or narrated story. Usually use two speakers. Keep the situation simple and useful, with short natural turns, questions, answers, clarifications, reactions, and a clear practical outcome. Avoid long speeches, artificial repetition, exaggerated slang, and vocabulary included only to teach a rule.

The body of a DIALOG must be actual direct speech, not prose describing that people talked. In every reading level, every one of the 4–5 paragraphs must contain labeled Hebrew speaker turns such as `נועה: ...` and `דני: ...`. Use at least two consistent speakers and alternate between them throughout the dialogue. Keep speaker names and the colon visible in the Hebrew text. Do not write third-person narrative paragraphs such as “נועה ראתה”, “היא שאלה”, “הם בדקו”, or “בסוף הם החליטו”. Put necessary context and actions naturally into what the speakers say. If the body is mainly narration, classify it as EVERYDAY rather than DIALOG.

Keep the speakers, situation, and outcome consistent across all reading levels. DIALOG stories use the same scenario metadata as EVERYDAY stories, have no sources or image, and must be visibly marked as fully AI-generated.
