# Shared generated-scenario diversity guide

Apply this guide to EVERYDAY, DIALOG, and individual SHORTS together.
Use the selected weekday topic list supplied in the request, including on retries and same-day appends.
The weekday themes are starting points for choosing topics, not recurring plots or mandatory quotas.
Keep the supplied canonical domains, pool targets, format contracts, and recent-scenario exclusions.
Choose fresh situations within the preferred topics first and assign the appropriate canonical domain.
Then use other underrepresented allowed domains when the preferred choices repeat recent material or do not cover the requested pool mix.
Do not reduce the requested batch to fit the topic list.

Prioritize common needs of adults who live in Israel and are learning Hebrew.
Before choosing each scenario, identify what a learner would need to say or understand in that situation.
If the answer is only describing a pleasant activity, choose a more useful situation.
Prefer paying, returning an item, explaining a problem, understanding a bill, arranging childcare, describing availability, or asking for directions.
Build the brief around an actual answer or action and the everyday spoken exchange that gets there.
Use situations where the reader needs to understand or say something at work, in a shop, at home, on transport, at school, or with a service provider.
Include asking someone to repeat, explaining a need, describing what happened, comparing options, and understanding an answer when they fit the situation.
Do not use shared calendars or organizing digital files as story topics.
Keep hobbies, workshops, cultural outings, and organized group activities occasional rather than central to the daily selection.
Experience domains have no reserved daily slots.
They may replace a targeted essential or routine item when they offer a distinct useful exchange and improve variety.
Cooking is one optional topic, not the default alternative to leisure activities or a recurring recipe adjustment.
Compare cooking scenarios across full stories and SHORTS, and favor another domain when recent issues already contain several.
For occasional experience stories, prefer ordinary meals and practical details during a journey over elaborate leisure plans.
An ordinary need can produce a useful story without a dramatic problem or an invented activity.

The publication weekday does not imply that an office, shop, or service is open that day.
Use a plausible fictional setting without asserting actual opening hours or official procedures.
Vary the activity within each theme from week to week.
Do not turn every Friday into a missing-ingredient story or every Tuesday into a repair call.

Treat `health_appointments`, `injuries_symptoms`, and `pharmacy_prescriptions` as one medical topic cluster when judging variety.
Medical vocabulary remains useful, but a clinic visit, symptom discussion, and prescription pickup do not provide three distinct themes for one issue.
Aim for at most one medical situation across the issue's full generated stories and SHORTS combined, counting already selected and existing items.
If recent records already contain a cluster of medical situations, choose non-medical topics for this batch.
Do not add a medical item simply because none has been selected today; medical topics are optional alternatives, not daily assignments.
Apply the same cluster comparison to repeated claims paperwork, document corrections, service calls, missing ingredients, and rescheduled appointments.
Choose a different activity when several recent stories share the same sequence, even if their domain labels differ.

Before drafting briefs, compare the recent and selected records by topic cluster, relationship, practical goal, action, and outcome.
Within a batch, mix cooperation, independent action, negotiation, discovery, explanation, and differing preferences.
Do not fill the essential pool with variations of customer asks → employee checks → customer confirms or waits.
For example, work and housing can involve dividing responsibilities, demonstrating a task, comparing needs, or preparing something together.
Use these as interaction categories, not fixed stories to repeat.
After choosing the batch, compare full stories and short items again so a short item does not retell a full story's situation.
Keep this comparison internal and return only the requested schema.
