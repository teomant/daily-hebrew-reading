# Shared generated-scenario diversity guide

Apply this guide to EVERYDAY, DIALOG, and individual SHORTS together.
Use the supplied topic list as the main direction, including on retries and same-day appends.
Choose fresh situations within those areas first.
Use another underrepresented allowed area only when the preferred areas repeat supplied material or cannot cover the requested pool mix.
Keep the canonical domains, pool targets, format contracts, and recent-scenario exclusions.
The areas are not recurring plots, mandatory quotas, or reasons to reduce a batch or discard a usable article.

Before drafting detailed briefs, choose each scenario's goal, relationship, interaction, and outcome in `everydayMeta.storyPattern`.
Describe these in compact English phrases without names, places, or replaceable objects.
Use the pattern to select the story, not just to summarize an already written brief.
Compare the proposed patterns with each other and with the supplied recent and selected records across formats.
If several differ mainly in objects or locations, change what happens before expanding their briefs.
A new domain or format alone does not make a new plot.
Keep useful repeated vocabulary while varying goals, relationships, actions, and results.

Choose situations by what a learner needs to say or understand in ordinary adult life in Israel.
Useful speech includes preferences, explanations, offers, refusals, reactions, and agreements as well as service requests.
People can cooperate, demonstrate a task, negotiate, act independently, or respond to a discovery.
These are possibilities, not a fixed list or daily rotation.
Neither a problem nor an employee who supplies the answer is required.
Do not build most of a collection around customer asks, employee checks, customer confirms or waits.
Give each situation a concrete action or exchange and an actual result.
Skip context-free pleasant moments and abstract decisions to start an activity.

Do not use shared calendars or organizing digital files as topics.
Keep hobbies, workshops, cultural outings, and organized group activities occasional.
Experience domains have no reserved daily slots.
They may replace an essential or routine target when they offer useful speech and improve variety.
Cooking is optional and must not become the default alternative.

Compare topic clusters as well as domain labels.
Treat `health_appointments`, `injuries_symptoms`, and `pharmacy_prescriptions` as one medical cluster.
Aim for at most one medical situation across full stories and SHORTS, including existing and selected items.
After a recent medical cluster, favor non-medical situations; medicine is not a daily assignment.
Also compare repeated paperwork, service calls, cooking adjustments, and appointment changes across formats.
Choose a different activity when recent or selected stories already concentrate on one sequence.

Expand the selected patterns into briefs that meet each format's length and interaction needs.
Keep each planned goal, relationship, and interaction clear enough to guide independent writing at every level.
Before returning the batch, check that a short item does not retell a full story or another short item.
Keep this comparison internal and return only the requested schema.
