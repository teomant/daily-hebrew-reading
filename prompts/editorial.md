# Sourced discovery instructions

Find factual CURRENT and HISTORY material for a practical spoken-Hebrew magazine. This phase only discovers and freezes supported English briefs and source metadata. Do not plan EVERYDAY or DIALOG stories, write Hebrew, choose reading-level wording, segment text, or translate anything.

Language value is the primary selection criterion: useful frequent vocabulary, relevance to conversations learners may actually have, a clear central idea, then freshness. CURRENT remains within Israel on every discovery pass. The first two HISTORY passes focus on Israeli subjects and repositories across varied cities, regions, communities, industries, and cultural fields. Search Hebrew as well as English. Do not default to Jerusalem, and do not let one city or English-language publisher dominate merely because its pages are easy to find. Only HISTORY may leave the Israeli collection scope on a final worldwide fallback; it must still use new queries and genuinely new subjects.

CURRENT can cover a person, culture, an industry or workplace, a location, a holiday or community event, a useful local change, or another concrete development from the target date or previous several days. It need not be a service notice. Look for genuinely interesting subjects people might discuss: food and prices, shops, work, housing, schools, local culture, recreation, community life, understandable human stories, and practical services. Transport disruption is only one possible topic, not a default. Technology is suitable when its human implications can be explained without specialist language.

For CURRENT, the central event, people, place, service, or institution must be in Israel and relevant to life here. An overseas event reported by an Israeli publisher is not local. Start from fresh Hebrew city and regional reporting such as mynet local editions, Hai Po, and Kolbo (colbonews.co.il); broaden across regions and practical beats using suitable Israeli consumer, food, culture, education, and community coverage, including Calcalist when relevant. Search municipal news and resident updates from cities such as Tel Aviv-Yafo, Haifa, Ashdod, and Be'er Sheva, plus relevant gov.il ministry and public-service announcements. These are discovery leads rather than quotas or a closed source list. Look across several publishers and regions; follow each lead to a canonical content page, check its date and facts, and prefer independent reporting for disputed claims. A municipal or ministry notice can support its own concrete announcement. Never cite a homepage, section page, search result, or undated listing as an article source.

The generator takes the first surviving CURRENT candidates for the issue. Put the strongest, most complementary CURRENT subjects first: each should offer a different practical situation and useful conversational vocabulary. Search across several everyday domains and publishers before ordering the pool. Do not lead with two near-identical service notices, such as bus diversions in different towns, when a distinct publishable subject is available. A different city, route, or date can make an event new without making the resulting reading experience varied. Compare recent issues for repeated article shapes as well as repeated events; seek a fresh practical angle instead of another version of yesterday's route-change or price-update story.

For each CURRENT brief, identify what actually happened or was reported, who or what it concerns, and concrete distinguishing details. Include a supported consequence or next step when one exists; a human-interest or cultural item need not claim an artificial practical impact. A notice that can only support “something changed; check for updates” is too thin for a full article. Keep briefs compact, factual, and limited to what the consulted source supports. Prioritize candidates whose essential facts can also be explained in short, concrete Alef sentences.

Never fill a CURRENT candidate slot with a placeholder, a report that no story was found, or commentary about the search. If fewer genuine CURRENT subjects are available, leave those slots unfilled rather than treating the absence of news as an article.

Avoid stories whose main value is spectacle or technical detail: distant natural disasters, rescue missions, space hardware, specialist science, and opaque business deals. Interesting products, industries, or discoveries are welcome when the article can discuss them in ordinary language. A story being surprising or widely reported is not enough; it must also make good Hebrew reading.

For CURRENT, exclude party politics, elections, politicians as protagonists, parliamentary or coalition disputes, ideological conflict, war, military operations, terrorism, geopolitics, diplomatic conflict, serious crime, murder, mass tragedy, outrage bait, and stories that require extensive political context. A historical politician may be the subject of HISTORY when the article concerns their past work or life and does not become a current partisan argument or war report.

For HISTORY on both Israel-focused attempts, search varied sources: Hebrew or English Wikipedia and Wikidata, the National Library of Israel and Historical Jewish Press, the Israel State Archives, National Photo Collection and PikiWiki Israel, the Israel Film Archive and Project Ben-Yehuda, and other suitable official, municipal, educational, archival, cultural, or biographical collections. Use a source page as a lead to the subject, not as the article topic. Historical people, including politicians, culture, industry, places, holidays and customs, institutions, objects, and events are all acceptable. A date connection is optional. Do not relabel current reporting as HISTORY. Worldwide history is permitted only when the user request explicitly marks the search as the final fallback after two Israeli passes.

Give every candidate a `historyFamily`. CURRENT uses `current`. HISTORY uses exactly one of `person`, `israeliIndustry`, `culture`, `event`, `place`, or `archaeology` according to its central subject. The label is descriptive, not a quota or daily rotation. A `person` article may be about a historical politician's life or work, but not today's political dispute; a newly published obituary or current death report is CURRENT, not HISTORY. `israeliIndustry` covers the history of an Israeli company, manufacturer, brand, cooperative, factory, trade, product, or industrial development. Do not use present-day startup funding rounds, valuations, product launches, or executive profiles as HISTORY. `culture` covers literature, music, theater, cinema, visual art, dance, design, architecture, food culture, publishing, broadcasting, or a cultural movement, work, or institution. A museum qualifies when its cultural creation, collection, or influence is the subject—not merely its building or visitor appeal. `event` covers past events, holidays, customs, education, infrastructure, transport, institutions, and ordinary objects.

Nature sites, national parks, gardens, trails, viewpoints, landmarks, fortresses, and tourist destinations are not a required HISTORY family. Return at most two `place` candidates, and only when each has distinctive researched historical facts that could not be retold by changing the location name. Reject generic park-preservation stories, travel-guide hooks, and “a place where nature and history meet” pitches. Archaeology is an even narrower fallback: return at most one `archaeology` candidate. Different place names do not make repeated excavation, old-building, museum-visit, fortress-visit, or park-history templates diverse.

Order HISTORY candidates by interest, specificity, factual substance, readability, and distinctness from the other candidates, not by family or search order. The first surviving candidates are selected. A place may be compelling, but rank a generic destination or excavation template below a specific human or cultural subject.

Among otherwise suitable HISTORY subjects, prefer concrete facts that an end-of-Alef reader can follow. An article may explain a person's work, an object, a custom, or several interesting facets of a place without a dramatic plot. A subject that depends on many unexplained institutions, specialist terms, or dates is a weaker choice even if its sources are strong.

Apply a strict recent-subject exclusion before returning anything. For HISTORY, the same named street, building, archaeological site, institution, person, event, object, custom, or other primary subject used in a forbidden or already selected story is a duplicate. A different source, excavation report, historical period, archaeological layer, newly emphasized fact, or angle about that same primary subject does not make it new. For CURRENT, a later status report, continuing consequence, “still” update, new article, or changed statistic about the same underlying event remains a duplicate. Discard such candidates and continue searching for a genuinely different primary subject or event.

Every candidate needs a compact factual English `brief` that identifies its central subject and underlying story for deduplication. CURRENT requires at least one consulted source page. For HISTORY, include a useful screening source when available, but an empty source list is acceptable because selected subjects receive a separate focused web-research pass after duplicate review and selection. This is screening, not deep research: do not return story beats.

Reject CURRENT event listings relabeled as HISTORY. A currently running exhibition, festival program, anniversary celebration, promotional institutional profile, or private collection is not HISTORY merely because it mentions an old person or organization. It may qualify when independent research supports a substantive article about the underlying historical subject.

Use real publisher names, article titles, and canonical HTTPS URLs. Every source URL must be unique and point to the content page itself. Never use a publisher homepage, section/category/tag page, search page, generic “latest” page, or rolling liveblog. Never invent names, quotations, reactions, numbers, causes, dates, outcomes, publishers, titles, or URLs.

When both sourced types are requested, return 18 compact candidate records: up to 6 genuine Israeli CURRENT candidates and at least 12 HISTORY candidates, using additional HISTORY subjects if CURRENT is scarce. If only CURRENT remains, return up to 18 genuine Israeli CURRENT candidates; fewer or none is better than a placeholder. If only HISTORY remains, return exactly 18 HISTORY candidates. Every record contains only `id`, `type`, `category`, `historyFamily`, `discoverySource`, `brief`, and `sources`. `discoverySource` is an internal claimed discovery route, not verified per-fact provenance. CURRENT uses `current`. Israel-focused HISTORY uses `wikimedia`, `nationalLibraryPress`, `stateVisualArchives`, `cultureArchives`, or `otherArchive` according to the collection that produced the lead; no route has a quota. A final worldwide HISTORY fallback uses `worldwideFallback`. Do not return `storyBeats`, adapt the candidates, write Hebrew, or return images.
