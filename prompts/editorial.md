# Sourced discovery instructions

Find factual CURRENT and HISTORY material for a practical spoken-Hebrew magazine. This phase only discovers and freezes supported English briefs and source metadata. Do not plan EVERYDAY or DIALOG stories, write Hebrew, choose reading-level wording, segment text, or translate anything.

Language value is the primary selection criterion: useful frequent vocabulary, relevance to conversations learners may actually have, a clear central idea, then freshness. The first two discovery passes focus on Israeli subjects and Israeli repositories across varied cities, regions, communities, industries, and cultural fields. Search Hebrew as well as English. Do not default to Jerusalem, and do not let one city or English-language publisher dominate merely because its pages are easy to find. Only a user request explicitly marked as the final worldwide fallback may leave the Israeli collection scope; it must still use new queries and genuinely new subjects.

CURRENT should focus on practical developments from the target date or previous several days that people might naturally discuss: food and prices, shops, services, public transport, workplaces, housing, appointments, schools, consumer rules, local culture, and understandable human stories. Prefer concrete effects on residents over national headline summaries. Technology is low priority and is suitable only when it directly changes how ordinary people use a common phone, app, payment, workplace, or service.

Reject stories whose main value is spectacle rather than everyday language: distant natural disasters, rescue missions, space hardware, specialist science, product-industry announcements, and technical business deals. A story being surprising or widely reported is not enough. Select it only if learners could realistically reuse much of its vocabulary in normal conversation.

Exclude party politics, elections, politicians as protagonists, parliamentary or coalition disputes, ideological conflict, war, military operations, terrorism, geopolitics, diplomatic conflict, serious crime, murder, mass tragedy, outrage bait, and stories that require extensive political context.

For HISTORY on both Israel-focused attempts, collect Israeli subjects through Hebrew or English Wikipedia and Wikidata, the National Library of Israel and Historical Jewish Press, the Israel State Archives, National Photo Collection and PikiWiki Israel, and the Israel Film Archive and Project Ben-Yehuda. Look first for worthwhile Israeli facts connected to the date when useful, but never force a date connection. The pool must be led by historical people, Israeli industry, and culture, supported by concrete events and ordinary-life history. A source page, archive record, photograph, museum object, newspaper result, or film record is a discovery lead—not the story itself. Follow it to concrete people, decisions, work, changes, conflicts, consequences, and outcomes. Do not relabel current reporting as HISTORY. Worldwide history is permitted only when the user request explicitly marks the search as the final fallback after two Israeli passes.

Give every candidate a `historyFamily`. CURRENT uses `current`. HISTORY uses exactly one of `person`, `israeliIndustry`, `culture`, `event`, `place`, or `archaeology` according to its central subject. When both sourced types are requested, build the 24-candidate HISTORY portion with at least six `person`, six `israeliIndustry`, six `culture`, and four `event` records. If only HISTORY remains, all 36 candidates are HISTORY and must preserve those minimums while using extra slots for the same preferred families. A `person` story centers on a specific historical person's life, work, decisions, and impact; a newly published obituary or current death report is CURRENT, not HISTORY. `israeliIndustry` covers the history of an Israeli company, manufacturer, brand, cooperative, factory, trade, product, or industrial development. Do not use present-day startups, high-tech unicorns, funding rounds, valuations, product launches, or executive profiles. `culture` covers literature, music, theater, cinema, visual art, dance, design, architecture, food culture, publishing, broadcasting, or a cultural movement, work, or institution. A museum qualifies only when its cultural creation, collection, or influence is the story—not merely its building or visitor appeal. `event` covers past events, customs, education, infrastructure, transport, institutions, and ordinary objects with a concrete human sequence and consequence.

Nature sites, national parks, gardens, trails, viewpoints, landmarks, fortresses, and tourist destinations are not a required HISTORY family. Return at most two `place` candidates, and only when each has an exceptional specific human story that could not be retold by changing the location name. Reject generic park-preservation stories, travel-guide hooks, and “a place where nature and history meet” pitches. Archaeology is an even narrower fallback: return at most one `archaeology` candidate. Different place names do not make repeated excavation, old-building, museum-visit, fortress-visit, or park-history templates diverse.

Order the first seven HISTORY candidates so they include at least two `person`, two `israeliIndustry`, two `culture`, and one `event` or exceptional `place`. No more than one of those seven may be `place`, and none may be `archaeology`. Rank specific people, companies, manufacturers, cultural works and movements, and consequential events ahead of generic destinations.

Apply a strict recent-subject exclusion before returning anything. For HISTORY, the same named street, building, archaeological site, institution, person, event, object, custom, or other primary subject used in a forbidden or already selected story is a duplicate. A different source, excavation report, historical period, archaeological layer, newly emphasized fact, or angle about that same primary subject does not make it new. For CURRENT, a later status report, continuing consequence, “still” update, new article, or changed statistic about the same underlying event remains a duplicate. Discard such candidates and continue searching for a genuinely different primary subject or event.

Every candidate needs a compact factual English `brief` that identifies its central subject and underlying story for deduplication. CURRENT requires at least one consulted source page. For HISTORY, include a useful screening source when available, but an empty source list is acceptable because selected subjects receive a separate focused web-research pass after duplicate review and selection. This is screening, not deep research: do not return story beats.

Reject CURRENT event listings relabeled as HISTORY. A currently running exhibition, festival program, anniversary celebration, promotional institutional profile, or private collection is not HISTORY merely because it mentions an old person or organization. It may qualify only when the central subject is an actual past sequence of people, decisions, changes, and outcomes that can later be researched independently.

Use real publisher names, article titles, and canonical HTTPS URLs. Every source URL must be unique and point to the content page itself. Never use a publisher homepage, section/category/tag page, search page, generic “latest” page, or rolling liveblog. Never invent names, quotations, reactions, numbers, causes, dates, outcomes, publishers, titles, or URLs.

Return exactly 36 compact candidate records for later deduplication and selection. When both sourced types are requested, return exactly 12 CURRENT and 24 HISTORY candidates. Every record contains only `id`, `type`, `category`, `historyFamily`, `discoverySource`, `brief`, and `sources`. `discoverySource` is an internal editorial lane, not verified per-fact provenance. CURRENT uses `current`. Israel-focused HISTORY uses `wikimedia`, `nationalLibraryPress`, `stateVisualArchives`, or `cultureArchives` according to the collection that produced the lead; a final worldwide fallback uses `worldwideFallback`. Do not return `storyBeats`, adapt the candidates, write Hebrew, or return images.
