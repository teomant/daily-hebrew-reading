# Editorial instructions

Create a practical spoken-Hebrew magazine, not a news digest. Its purpose is to help learners talk to real people in streets, offices, shops, services, homes, and social situations. Language value is the primary criterion: useful frequent vocabulary, realistic conversational relevance, explainability for beginners, a clear central idea, then freshness.

For a new complete issue, aim for 4 CURRENT, 3 EVERYDAY, 3 DIALOG, and 2 HISTORY stories, but treat these as editorial targets rather than mandatory counts. Review the supplied summaries from previous issues and do not repeat the same real event, historical subject, everyday plot, or dialogue situation on consecutive days. If a good unique CURRENT or HISTORY candidate is unavailable or rejected as a duplicate, fill that slot with an EVERYDAY or DIALOG story instead.

CURRENT is a substantial part of the issue, normally 4 stories and sometimes 5. The problem to avoid is irrelevant news, not news itself. Prefer practical developments people might naturally discuss during an ordinary day: food and prices, shops, services, public transport, workplaces, housing, appointments, schools, consumer rules, local culture, and understandable human stories. At least half of CURRENT should concern Israel or a specific Israeli city or community when suitable material exists. Prefer local reporting and concrete effects on residents over national headline summaries. Technology is low priority and is suitable only when it directly changes how ordinary people use a common phone, app, payment, workplace, or service.

Reject stories whose main value is spectacle rather than everyday language: natural disasters far away, rescue missions, space hardware or telescope operations, specialist science, product-industry announcements, and technical business deals. A story being surprising or widely reported is not enough. Select it only if learners could realistically reuse much of its vocabulary in normal conversation.

Exclude party politics, elections, politicians as protagonists, parliamentary or coalition disputes, ideological conflict, war, military operations, terrorism, geopolitics, diplomatic conflict, serious crime, murder, mass tragedy, outrage bait, and stories that require extensive political context.

HISTORY is normally 2 stories. Prioritize local, relatable history: the history of an Israeli street, neighborhood, building, station, market, institution, food, custom, or public place; an event connected to an approaching Jewish or Israeli holiday; or a past event whose effects remain visible in ordinary life. Broader Israeli or world history is acceptable when it is easy to tell and supplies reusable vocabulary. It may be on this day or seasonally relevant, but never force a dull date connection.

Each sourced story must contain a concise factual brief. Every fact in every reading level must be supported by that brief. Never invent names, quotations, reactions, numbers, causes, dates, or outcomes. When evidence is thin, write less. Prefer real publisher names, article titles, and canonical HTTPS URLs, but return an empty source list when no trustworthy canonical URL is available. Every returned source URL must be unique within the batch and point to the specific article, research paper, museum object, or other content page used for that story. Never use a publisher homepage, section/category/tag page, search page, generic “latest” page, or rolling liveblog as a source. Images are optional: only return a direct HTTPS image URL when it comes from a listed source, has clear credit, and a rights or usage-policy page explicitly permits this use. Store that consulted policy URL and a short factual rights label; never guess a license. Otherwise return no image.

Write every internal story brief in English. Treat the supplied forbidden-story records as a strict semantic exclusion list. Compare candidate subjects, briefs, slugs, and source URLs against it, and do not repeat the same story merely because another language, publisher, URL, headline, wording, angle, or extra detail is available.
