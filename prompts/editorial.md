# Sourced discovery instructions

Find factual CURRENT and HISTORY material for a practical spoken-Hebrew magazine. This phase only discovers and freezes supported English briefs and source metadata. Do not plan EVERYDAY or DIALOG stories, write Hebrew, choose reading-level wording, segment text, or translate anything.

Language value is the primary selection criterion: useful frequent vocabulary, relevance to conversations learners may actually have, a clear central idea, then freshness. Search across the whole of Israel and varied cities, regions, and communities. Do not default to Jerusalem, and do not let one city dominate merely because its English-language sources are easy to find.

CURRENT should focus on practical developments from the target date or previous several days that people might naturally discuss: food and prices, shops, services, public transport, workplaces, housing, appointments, schools, consumer rules, local culture, and understandable human stories. Prefer concrete effects on residents over national headline summaries. Technology is low priority and is suitable only when it directly changes how ordinary people use a common phone, app, payment, workplace, or service.

Reject stories whose main value is spectacle rather than everyday language: distant natural disasters, rescue missions, space hardware, specialist science, product-industry announcements, and technical business deals. A story being surprising or widely reported is not enough. Select it only if learners could realistically reuse much of its vocabulary in normal conversation.

Exclude party politics, elections, politicians as protagonists, parliamentary or coalition disputes, ideological conflict, war, military operations, terrorism, geopolitics, diplomatic conflict, serious crime, murder, mass tragedy, outrage bait, and stories that require extensive political context.

For HISTORY, look first for worthwhile Israeli facts connected to the target date or recent days, but never force a date connection. Also search for short, interesting historical subjects from anywhere in Israel: a street, neighborhood, building, station, market, institution, food, custom, public place, everyday object, transport route, local business, or ordinary practice. Broader Israeli history is acceptable when it is easy to tell and supplies reusable vocabulary. World history is only a fallback. Do not relabel current reporting as HISTORY.

Every selected story needs a concise factual brief supported by the consulted source page. Never invent names, quotations, reactions, numbers, causes, dates, or outcomes. When evidence is thin, include less. Prefer real publisher names, article titles, and canonical HTTPS URLs, but return an empty source list when no trustworthy canonical URL is available. Every source URL must be unique and point to the specific content page used. Never use a publisher homepage, section/category/tag page, search page, generic “latest” page, or rolling liveblog.

Images are optional. Return one only when it comes from a listed source, has clear credit, and a rights or usage-policy page explicitly permits this use. Store that consulted policy URL and a short factual rights label; never guess a license. Otherwise return no image.
