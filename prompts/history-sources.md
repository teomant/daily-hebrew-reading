# HISTORY source guide

This guide applies only to HISTORY. Every subject must have an explicit Israeli or Jewish connection. First and second discovery passes seek Israeli subjects; only the final fallback expands to Jewish communities, people, and culture outside Israel. A source's location or Jewish publisher alone does not establish that connection. State the connection in the existing factual brief.

## Evidence priorities

Start with subject-specific curated museum/library collections, scholarly interpretation, and documented original material. Compare several relevant collections before ranking candidates; do not fill the top positions from one familiar blog or from generic biographies. Prefer an explanation of how people lived, worked, cooked, learned, communicated, or created something, with concrete supported details that readers can retell in everyday Hebrew. These are priorities, not family or publisher quotas.

Use the following concrete routes. The URLs are starting points, not automatic article citations or preselected subjects. Search with a fresh subject in Hebrew and English where useful; open individual records and follow their references. Never turn a collection landing page into the topic of an article.

On the final Jewish-history fallback, the existing output label is `worldwideFallback` regardless of which collection provides the lead. On Israeli passes, classify other actual routes using the existing labels; a label is not proof of reliability.

Wikipedia and Wikidata remain useful for orientation, spelling variants, dates to investigate, and bibliography. Search `site:he.wikipedia.org` or `site:en.wikipedia.org` with the subject, follow cited sources, and evaluate those sources rather than assuming the reference is sound. Do not ban useful Wikipedia material, but prefer attributable specialist evidence for defining facts. NLI blog articles, reputable reporting, state/municipal archives, and university histories remain supplementary routes; their institutional name alone does not make every claim authoritative. A company history can document its own activity but is weak evidence for unqualified “first,” “best,” or impact claims.

## Research discipline and access

Look for an independent supporting account for disputed dates, dramatic anecdotes, and “first” claims. Several pages copying the same account are one evidence chain. If a claim cannot be supported, omit it or preserve a supported qualification; never invent precision or combine conflicting versions into a new fact. Return only consulted content-page URLs and connect factual beats to those sources where links are available. Source links remain optional metadata, but researched evidence is required; an inaccessible page or a search snippet cannot supply missing facts.

Access observations from 2026-10-18: MUZA, Film Archive, Ben-Yehuda, and Posen returned readable text; Posen may restrict full entries. NLI collection pages were indexed but direct fetches sometimes returned 403. JTA's archive returned 403, JWA's encyclopedia showed a bot challenge, and YIVO's encyclopedia required JavaScript; these are conditional leads, not dependable evidence in this environment. If a page is inaccessible, seek an accessible authoritative account or choose another subject. Do not bypass access controls or pretend to have read it. Availability can change; collection URLs are navigation aids, never an instruction to copy unverified source metadata.
